What the name builder returns

The seven scoring parts, the grades and the sound check.

Every name you build is scored out of 100 in seven parts, and each part has a fixed weight: Useful god 30 (does each character carry an element your baby's BaZi chart needs: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal or Water), Five Grids (五格) 25 (the five stroke-count grids), Three Talents (三才) 15 (whether the elements of the heaven, person and earth grids support each other), character meaning (字义) 10 (what the characters mean, in 16 meaning categories), sound (音韵) 5 (tones, repeated sounds and unlucky homophones), gender fit (性别) 5 (whether the characters suit a boy or a girl) and confidence (可信度) 10 (how far the engine can trust the data behind the other parts). The weights add up to 100.

The total maps to a grade: excellent (上佳) at 90 and above, good (佳) at 80 and above, fair (可) at 65 and above, and needs a second look (待斟酌) below 65. A strong sound hit caps the grade at needs a second look whatever the total.

The sound layer checks two things. It looks for Mandarin sound-alikes, characters that sound like an unlucky or rude word, and it checks the English reading of the pinyin, since many Singapore and Malaysia families raise children who will be addressed in English at school and at work. Beside the score you also get the Five Grids with their auspicious (吉) or adverse (凶) verdicts, the element of every character, its Kangxi (康熙) stroke count, a short reading of what the name suggests, and a shortlist of up to eight names you can compare.

How Singapore and Malaysia names are handled

Romanised surnames, Kangxi strokes and Singapore time.

Families in Singapore and Malaysia usually know their surname by a Hokkien, Teochew or Cantonese romanisation such as Tan, Lim, Lee, Ng or Wong, while the scoring needs the Chinese character. Enter the Chinese surname you choose in the surname box (for example 王, 林, 李 or 吴) and the builder uses that character for the stroke count and the grids. The romanised spelling on a passport is a spelling of the character, not a different surname, so pick the character your family writes.

The Five Grids use the Kangxi stroke count from the Kangxi dictionary, which counts the traditional form of each character. The traditional 祿 has more strokes than the simplified 禄, so the builder counts the traditional form and shows you the strokes it used. The chart is calculated for Singapore time with a true-solar correction, and a due date gives a provisional chart that you can recalculate after birth.
