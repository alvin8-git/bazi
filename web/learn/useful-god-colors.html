用神: Your Favourable Elements — and the Colours That Carry Them

2026-09-16 · bazifor.me Learn · BaZi Basics

After the Four Pillars are cast and your Day Master's strength is assessed, classical BaZi asks the most practical question in the whole system: which element does this chart need? That element is the 用神 — usually translated "useful god," though "operative element" is closer to the sense. Everything actionable in a reading — colours, directions, timing, even room choices in home fengshui — flows from it.

How the 用神 is actually chosen

The classical toolkit has four techniques, applied in order:

  1. 扶抑 (support–restrain) — the workhorse. A weak Day Master is supported by the element that generates it (印, the resource) and the element that matches it (比劫, peers); a strong one is drained or disciplined by output (食伤), wealth (财), or authority (官杀).
  2. 调候 (climate adjustment) — a chart born in deep winter may need fire regardless of strength; a blazing summer chart may need water. Season overrides arithmetic when the imbalance is extreme.
  3. 病药 (illness and medicine) — when one element dominates and distorts the chart, the element that checks it is the cure.
  4. 通关 (bridging) — when two camps deadlock, the element that mediates between them unlocks the chart.

These are written rules with a defined order of precedence. Two practitioners applying them honestly reach the same answer — which is why a deterministic engine can compute your 用神 and cite the technique it used.

From elements to colours

Each element carries a colour family, and this is the oldest lifestyle application of BaZi:

  • Wood (木) — greens and teals
  • Fire (火) — reds, oranges, purples
  • Earth (土) — yellows, beige, browns, terracotta
  • Metal (金) — white, silver, grey, metallics, gold tones
  • Water (水) — blues and black

Your favourable elements' colours are worth favouring where you spend sustained time — wardrobe staples, bedding, a study corner, a phone case you see two hundred times a day. Your unfavourable elements' colours (忌神) are not poison; they are simply not doing you any favours in bulk. The classical advice is dosage, not prohibition: an unfavourable colour as an accent is background noise; as the whole bedroom wall, it is a standing weight.

Where people go wrong

  • Guessing from the Day Master alone. A water Day Master does not automatically "need" water — a strong 壬 usually needs the opposite.
  • Treating the zodiac animal as the answer. The year branch is one-eighth of the chart.
  • Applying colours without the room's own context. In home fengshui, a room's flying-star overlay can override the personal palette on walls and large surfaces, with the personal colours moving to textiles. Rules interact; a good reading shows the precedence.
