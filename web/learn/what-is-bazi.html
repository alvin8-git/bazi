What Is BaZi? The Four Pillars, Explained Without Mystery

2026-09-16 · bazifor.me Learn · BaZi Basics

BaZi (八字, "eight characters") is often described as Chinese astrology, but that framing hides what it actually is: a calendar computation. Your birth moment is converted into four pairs of characters — the Four Pillars (四柱) — using the traditional Chinese solar calendar. There is no interpretation involved in this first step at all. Two people born in the same place at the same minute get the same eight characters, every time, from every competent practitioner.

The four pillars

Each pillar is one Heavenly Stem (天干) sitting on one Earthly Branch (地支):

  • Year pillar (年柱) — the stem and branch of your birth year, switching at 立春 (the "start of spring" solar term, around 4 February), not at Chinese New Year. This is the most common mistake in casual zodiac readings.
  • Month pillar (月柱) — set by solar terms, so each "month" begins at an astronomical moment, not a calendar date.
  • Day pillar (日柱) — the 60-day 甲子 cycle that has run unbroken for millennia. The day stem is your Day Master (日主) — the character that represents you in the chart.
  • Hour pillar (时柱) — one of twelve two-hour blocks (时辰), corrected to true solar time for your birthplace, because the sky does not care about time zones.

Ten stems and twelve branches combine into a 60-unit cycle (六十甲子), so the same day pillar recurs every 60 days and the same year pillar every 60 years.

What the characters carry

Every stem and branch maps to one of the Five Elements (五行 — wood, fire, earth, metal, water) and a yin or yang polarity. Branches additionally contain hidden stems (藏干), which is why two charts with similar surfaces can behave very differently. From these mappings, classical technique derives everything else: the strength of your Day Master, your favourable elements (用神), the Ten Gods (十神) that describe how the other characters relate to you, and the ten-year luck cycles (大运) that sequence a life.

Common corrections worth knowing

  1. The year boundary is 立春, not January 1st and not Chinese New Year.
  2. The hour pillar needs true solar time — in some cities the clock is more than half an hour away from the sun, enough to shift a 时辰.
  3. Historical clock changes matter. Singapore, for example, ran on UTC+7:30 before 1982 — a birth time from that era must be corrected before the pillars are cast.
  4. A chart is a starting point, not a verdict. The same pillars read differently once luck cycles and annual years (流年) are layered on.
