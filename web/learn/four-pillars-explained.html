How to Read a Four Pillars Chart: Stems, Branches, Hidden Stems and the Four Palaces

The reference our readings link to from the 四柱 Pillars tab.

A BaZi chart is eight characters arranged in four columns — the four pillars (四柱) of the year, month, day and hour of birth. Each pillar is written as two characters: a heavenly stem (天干) on top and an earthly branch (地支) below. The stem is the visible, declared energy of that pillar; the branch is its foundation, and the year branch carries the zodiac animal most people know their birth year by.

The Day Master — the character that is you

The stem of the day pillar is the 日主 Day Master: the character the whole chart is read against. Every other character is classified by its relationship to it — those relationships are the ten gods. The day pillar is usually drawn outlined in a chart for this reason: its stem is you, and its branch is the spouse palace (配偶宮), the seat of marriage and domestic life.

Hidden stems — the ingredients inside each branch

Every branch stores one to three hidden stems (藏干): extra elements sealed inside the pillar like ingredients in a pantry. 亥 hides 壬 water and 甲 wood; 辰 hides 戊 earth, 乙 wood and 癸 water. A chart that looks light in some element on the surface may carry it hidden in the branches — which is why serious element counts always weigh hidden stems, and why a Day Master with no visible support can still be "rooted" (通根) if its element hides in a branch below.

The four pillars are also the four palaces

Classical doctrine reads the same four columns a second way, as the 宮位 four palaces — a timeline and a map of relationships at once:

  • Year pillar 年柱 — ages 0–15 · ancestry, childhood environment, first impressions.
  • Month pillar 月柱 — ages 16–32 · parents, early adulthood, and the career headquarters: the month is the strongest single influence on the chart's climate.
  • Day pillar 日柱 — ages 33–48 · the self (stem) and the spouse (branch).
  • Hour pillar 時柱 — ages 49+ · children, students, works, late-life luck.

So the ten gods say WHAT an energy is; the palace it sits in says WHEN it peaks and WHERE it plays out. A wealth god in the month palace colours the career; the same god in the hour palace colours the later years and the children. Whenever a luck decade or a passing year clashes or combines with a pillar, it is that palace's life-area that feels it.

What else a pillar carries

Each pillar also has a 納音 melodic element — the poetic sixty-cycle name like 屋上土 "earth on the roof" — and a 長生 life-stage, the Day Master's phase in that pillar (birth → peak → decline). These are flavour and vitality readings layered over the structure, not verdicts. Symbolic stars (神煞) such as 天乙貴人 the Nobleman or 文昌 the scholar star also attach to specific pillars, and are read in the palace they land in: a Nobleman in the year palace means helpful elders early in life.

Read in this order — Day Master first, then the month for climate, then each palace — a four pillars chart stops being eight mysterious characters and becomes a structured record: who, in what season, supported or drained by what, and when each theme takes the stage.
