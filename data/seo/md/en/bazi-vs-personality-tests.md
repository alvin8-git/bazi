A personality questionnaire asks how you describe yourself, and a BaZi chart is computed from when and where you were born. They take different inputs and answer different questions, so a fair comparison is about method, not about which one wins.

## What is the difference in method?

A questionnaire such as MBTI, Big Five or DISC takes your answers as its input. By construction, it measures how you describe yourself on the day you answer. Self-report questionnaires can give different results on retest (method note), because mood, circumstances and self-image change.

The BaZi engine takes birth data: date, time, place and sex. Its computation is repeatable. The same birth data always gives the same chart, which is the first of the ten method claims published at /method. The engine converts the clock to true solar time, then casts the pillars. Take the sample, 王大明, a fictional man born 1988-03-15 at 09:30 in Singapore. His clock time becomes 08:16 and his pillars are 戊辰 (Earth Dragon) 乙卯 (Wood Rabbit) 己巳 (Earth Snake) 戊辰.

Repeatable is not the same as valid. Whether a chart maps to character is a separate claim. This site makes no claim that BaZi personality readings have been validated by psychology research (method note). The engine states only that it computes classical structures and gives their traditional reading.

Key facts

- A questionnaire takes self-report as input, and the BaZi engine takes birth date, time, place and sex.

## What does each approach do well?

A questionnaire gives you a shared vocabulary for how you see yourself now. That suits team conversations and self-reflection at a particular moment. Its limits are the ones above: it records your self-image, and your self-image moves.

The BaZi engine does three things a questionnaire does not try to do.

- It is stable. The chart is a fixed reference built from birth data, not a snapshot of mood.

- It is structural. It expresses strengths and gaps as element balances and names favourable elements. The sample is score -0.97 (身弱), and the engine lists Fire (火) and Earth (土) as favourable.

- It has a time axis. It grades each ten-year luck cycle and flags annual windows. The sample's 己未 decade (ages 37-47) is graded growth, and his 辛酉 (Metal Rooster) decade (ages 57-67) is graded corrective.

The same chart also drives other layers, such as the Eight House directions and date selection. For the sample, the Eight House type is 震命 (kua 3, East group), the East group. One birth record feeds personality notes, timing and home layout.

## What should neither approach claim?

Neither should claim to settle who you are. A questionnaire type is a summary of answers. A chart is a map of classical structure. The engine says it does not predict events, and it records choices where schools differ and marks them as disputed.

A careful reader treats both as lenses.

## How can you use both together?

Use the questionnaire for what it records: your current self-description. Use the chart for what it computes: structure and timing. Then compare.

- Write down the questionnaire result and the date you took it.

- Open your chart and note the eight axes and their stated bases.

- Mark where the two agree and where they differ.

- For each difference, ask whether circumstances are masking the structure, or whether the chart's reading simply does not fit you.

Either answer is useful. A mismatch is a question, not an error. Because the chart's rules are written down, you can test a verdict against your own life and decide it does not apply. That is a feature of a rule-based reading.

## What changes for Singapore and Malaysia readers?

A BaZi comparison is only as good as the birth data, so local time rules matter more than for a questionnaire.

- Year boundary. The BaZi year turns at Start of Spring (立春), not on 1 January. In 2027, Start of Spring falls on 2027-02-04. A birth in late January may belong to the earlier year, and that changes the pillars and every axis downstream.

- Time zone. Singapore ran on UTC+7:30 before 1982-01-01 and UTC+8 after it. Malaysia has used UTC+8 since 1982-01-01. The method page states that clock times before 1982 use the offset of that era.

- True solar time. The engine adjusts for longitude and the equation of time. The sample's 09:30 became 08:16, using longitude 103.8517°E.

- Unknown hour. If the hour is uncertain, run the chart with a few candidate times and see whether the hour pillar changes. If it does not, the doubt does not matter.

## FAQ

### Is BaZi more accurate than MBTI?

The two take different inputs, so accuracy is not a fair comparison. A questionnaire records how you describe yourself on the day. BaZi computes a repeatable chart from birth data. Repeatable is not the same as valid, and no psychology validation is claimed.

### Why does my MBTI result change when I retake it?

Self-report questionnaires can give different results on retest, because mood, circumstances and self-image change. A BaZi chart does not move this way. The same birth data always gives the same chart.

### What birth details do I need for a BaZi chart in Singapore or Malaysia?

You need the birth date, time, place and sex. Singapore ran on UTC+7:30 before 1982-01-01 and UTC+8 after it. Malaysia has used UTC+8 since 1982-01-01. Local time rules matter more here than for a questionnaire.

### Does BaZi predict events or tell me who I am?

No. The engine says it does not predict events, and no generative AI writes any reading. A chart is a map of classical structure. Neither a chart nor a questionnaire settles who you are, so treat both as lenses.

### Can I use a personality test and a BaZi chart together?

Yes. Write down your questionnaire result and date, then note the chart's eight axes and their stated bases. Mark where they agree and differ. A mismatch is a question, not an error.
